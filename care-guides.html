Plant Care Guides

Whether you're caring for your first houseplant or managing a full garden, these simple guides will help you keep your plants healthy and thriving.

Watering Basics

Most plants prefer consistent moisture but dislike sitting in water. Always check the top inch of soil before watering and ensure your pots have proper drainage.

Light Requirements

Understanding light levels is key to plant success. South-facing windows offer bright, direct light, while north-facing windows provide gentle, indirect light ideal for shade‑loving plants.

Soil & Fertilizer

Use high‑quality organic soil blends and feed your plants with natural fertilizers during the growing season for best results.

Humidity & Temperature

Many houseplants come from tropical environments, which means they appreciate warm temperatures and moderate humidity. Keep plants away from cold drafts, heating vents, and air conditioners. For humidity‑loving plants, consider grouping them together or placing a small tray of water nearby to increase moisture in the air.

Repotting Tips

Plants outgrow their pots over time. If you notice roots circling the bottom, soil drying out too quickly, or slowed growth, it may be time to repot. Choose a pot 1–2 inches larger than the current one and refresh the soil to give your plant room to grow.

Pest Prevention

Common pests like gnats, aphids, and spider mites can appear even in clean homes. Check your plants regularly for sticky leaves, webbing, or tiny insects. Wipe leaves with a damp cloth and use gentle, plant‑safe sprays like neem oil to keep pests under control.

Pruning & Maintenance

Regular pruning helps plants stay healthy and encourages new growth. Remove yellowing leaves, trim leggy stems, and pinch back new growth to shape your plant. Always use clean, sharp scissors to avoid damaging the plant.

Choosing the Right Pot

The type of pot you choose affects how your plant grows. Terracotta pots help soil dry faster, making them great for succulents and cacti. Plastic pots retain moisture longer, which is ideal for tropical plants. No matter the style, always choose pots with drainage holes.

Beginner‑Friendly Plants

If you're new to plant care, start with easy, low‑maintenance plants like pothos, snake plants, spider plants, or ZZ plants. These varieties tolerate a wide range of conditions and are very forgiving while you learn.
